### Task 1 probes (2026-11-23, this PC at 1920×1080; live tests behind `KX_LIVE_SNIP=1`)

- UI limits: both the record job and the Save dialog's job worked under the worker's full UI limits (`job.rs` `UI`), so neither job needs a limit lifted.
- Shield timing: a window shielded before it first shows was never in a GDI grab (31 frames from its show on), and showed once unshielded. A window already shown left the grab at once: the next grab after the shield call, 5–8 ms, no frame waited, in all 25 rounds over five runs.
- Frame rate: the whole 1920×1020 work area recorded at 30 fps with no late frame, here and in weak mode (2 cores at 30 %); 89 of 90 frames were new captures.
- Keep frames on the graphics card: copying each frame through the processor held 30 fps at 1280×720 in weak mode, but at the full work area 12 and then 60 of 90 frames came late. Handing the textures to Media Foundation through its DXGI device manager fixed that and cut peak memory from 142–204 MB to 95 MB.
- Media Foundation: the Sink Writer chose a hardware H.264 encoder. Each file read back as 3008 ms, H.264 at 30/1 with 90 frames, and AAC (48 kHz stereo, 192 kbit/s); a decoded frame showed our backdrop where the shielded window was.

Of the 144 000 frames in 3 s, the microphone gave 142 080–142 400 and the PC's sound 142 079–142 399 (it gives none while nothing plays); a missing device reads absent, a failed stream refused.
- Sound gaps: the PC sound's stream reported one gap (cpal's xrun, a WASAPI discontinuity) in every recording and went on. The probe counts gaps and fails a source only on other errors, and the `record` job keeps that split.
- Memory: a full-work-area recording peaked at 95–97 MB, well under the planned `record_mb` of 512; the dialog's worker peaked at 11 MB of its own memory.
- Save dialog: it opened from the worker owned by a hidden topmost window of the worker, in front of and above our topmost "editor" window, and returned the exact path after a click message to its own Save button.

- Built (tasks 8b and 15): Snip's windows leave the topmost band while a dialog shows (the view's `dialog`), the keyboard stays above so the user can type, and the app grants the worker the foreground with `AllowSetForegroundWindow`. The hands-on check confirms both on a dev-install.

### Task 6 decisions (2026-11-24)

- DPI: the record and dialog jobs make their own work thread per-monitor aware (`SetThreadDpiAwarenessContext`), so they read a part in physical pixels as the app gives it. A manifest would change every job of the worker; the voice, media, vault and fetch jobs never call it and keep the default.
- Media Foundation loads only when a video job asks: the worker delay-loads `mfplat.dll` and `mfreadwrite.dll` (named once, in `kx-platform-record/src/record.toml`), and the job loads them from the system folder before its first call, answering `NoVideo` when they are missing (edge 14). A worker test checks its start-up imports name neither.
- The encoder's fixed values live in that table, not in the settings: the video bitrate, the AAC rate, the first-frame wait, the capture's buffers, the sound queue, the start cushion, the drift allowed and the downmix of each channel count.
- Sound is placed on the video's timeline by when it was captured, so picture and sound stay in step across Mic and Sound switches (tested over fake sources, within one sound frame). A switched-off source records silence, and one that stops by itself is told with a new `RecordLost` note and records silence too while the run goes on; `cpal` gaps are counted, not fatal.
- A run's or a copy's last note (its end, or the fault that ended it) is handed back after every part closed, so the job is free for the next ask before the host reads that note.
- A run that falls behind (a slow encoder) skips to the current slot, so its picture holds a frame or two and never drifts from the sound; its times and the longest length stay right. A sound switched on still opens in the slot loop, which now costs only such a held frame.
- Speed copies place each frame by its own time at half or a quarter of it, one a slot, and average the sound, so they play faster and higher in step, even from a run that skipped slots.
- Every time and length told is capped at the longest video, as Media Foundation reads a file a few ms longer than told.
- A run or copy that cannot delete its broken video, once every part and Media Foundation closed, ends in `Failed`, even after Cancel; the host then deletes the file.
- The capture reads the pointer and border back and refuses a start without them, so every live run checks both.
- Live (`KX_LIVE_SNIP=1`, this PC at 1920×1020 work area, Mic and Sound on): the `record` job told its time at 0, 1, 2 and 3 s, 3.003 s apart on the wall clock, here and in weak mode (3.001 s). Each video read back as 3050 ms, H.264 at 30/1 with 91 frames, AAC with 146 432 sound frames, and our backdrop where the shielded window was.
- Live copies: 2× gave 1535 ms with 46 frames; 4× of a one-frame run gave a playable 42 ms file with one frame and sound (edge 20); a cancelled run left no file.
- Live memory: the record job peaked at 103 MB recording alone (weak mode), and at 301 MB after two 1080p copies, which decode on the processor; both under the planned `record_mb` of 512.
- The first live run found a copy bug no fake could: the reel took the reader's "first stream" constants for its stream numbers, so a copy never ended. A test now writes, reads back and copies a small real video with no screen or device.

### Task 7 findings (2026-11-24)

- The `pick` job replaced the probe. Its answer rule lives once in `kx-ipc` as `PickAsk::check`, since tidy keeps `kx-platform-record` out of the app: at most `MAX_PATH_BYTES` (what the reply frame carries), a drive letter, a colon and a separator, then parts that are plain file names within 255 UTF-16 units, none ending in a dot or a space (Win32 trims those, so `.`, `..` and aliases fail); a Save answer must also end in the asked type. UNC, `\\?\`, `\\.\` and relative paths fail.
- Replies: `Picked` (already checked; the host repeats `ask.check(&path)` before any write), `Cancelled`, `Refused` (the user chose a place the check refuses, such as a network place, which the host maps to `BadPath`) and `Failed { code }` (no dialog showed or its answer was unreadable; the code is the `HRESULT`, `NO_CODE` for a panic, safe to log).
- A mapped network drive (`Z:\` on a share) passes, as the check is lexical; this is accepted, as the user chose that place (task 7 review).
- Both dialogs set `FOS_FORCEFILESYSTEM`, `FOS_NOCHANGEDIR` and `FOS_DONTADDTORECENT`; Save adds `FOS_OVERWRITEPROMPT` and `FOS_STRICTFILETYPES`, with one `*.png` or `*.mp4` filter as its label, so no text needs translating. The dialog thread is per-monitor DPI aware, and a dialog asked at a folder that is gone opens where Windows chooses instead of failing.
- Live (`KX_LIVE_SNIP=1`, 6 runs): the Save dialog opened in front of and above our topmost stand-in as the foreground window; Save answered `<folder>\<Arabic name>.png` with no file written, a Save at a missing folder opened and its Cancel answered `Cancelled`, and the folder dialog answered its folder. The pick job peaked at 11–12 MB, and the z-band caveat above still waits for the hands-on check.
- `Encode` runs in the media job: PNG 8-bit RGB through `flate2` at level 1, which falls back to stored blocks, so noise stays within its bound; `fdeflate` grew past it on 2048² noise. The PNG buffer is reserved once at `png_most` of its RGB rows (height × (width × 3 + 1)), about 3/4 of the pixels, and our row buffer before the first row, so a job too small answers `TooBig`.
- Encode memory, measured through `WorkerPictures` on 8192² pixels (256 MiB, `shot_cap_mb`'s default) in a worker under its job: peak 455 MiB for noise (PNG 192 MiB, 4.0 s, read past the usual 64 MiB bound) and for a screen-like picture (PNG 1 MiB, 1.4 s). The snip module sizes each `Encode`'s `memory_mb` from its pixels plus `png_most` of its RGB rows, and its `deadline_ms` from its size (task 13).
- The host reads an encode's PNG within `png_most` of the pixels sent, and every other reply within the usual 64 MiB: the piped worker sets each ask's bound when sent and takes it when the reply's first byte comes. The app holds the shot and one copy of its pixels while it sends, about 2 × raw for a moment.

### Task 8a findings (2026-11-24)

- The freeze's steps are OS-neutral: `kx_platform::snip::Freezer` reads an adapter's `Desk` (screens, cap, one grab of every screen, windows, pointer). `WinScreenFreeze` is the Windows desk, run with its thread per-monitor aware, so pixels are physical whatever the app's manifest; `WindowsPlatform::screen_freeze()` hands it to the app.
- Each screen is its whole monitor, taskbar included, copied by GDI `BitBlt` with `CAPTUREBLT` through one screen DC. No screen gives an empty `Frozen`, as the fake; an unreadable pointer reads as the desktop's origin, on the main screen.
- Cap (edge 4): the screens' raw bytes together, checked before any grab. Past it, `TooBig` and nothing grabbed; a buffer with no room is `Refused(E_OUTOFMEMORY)`.
- Window list (edge 3): top-level windows front first, offered only when shown, not ours, not the shell's desktop, not minimised, not a tool window and not cloaked, with a non-empty DWM frame (physical, no shadow). Our own Qt windows are tool windows too.
- The crop maths went to `kx-module-api` (`Part::spanning`, `Part::cut`), not `kx-platform`, since the snip module cuts pictures and may depend on `kx-module-api` alone. A cut paints a pixel on no screen black, and refuses a part on no screen or bigger than the frozen screens together, so no picture outgrows `shot_cap_mb`.
- `shield` covers every top-level window of ours with that title, shown or not, and reads it back; an older Windows that keeps another affinity gives `Refused`, so the caller steps aside. The window must exist with its title first, and each call walks every top-level window (about 320 here), so call it once per change.
- `write_kept` writes the same formats as `write` with no marker at all, not even the cloud switch, so our watch keeps a snip, and Windows' history and its cloud sync a picture, never a video's file drop (ADR-0056).
- Live (`KX_LIVE_SNIP=1`, 3 runs, 1920×1080 at 125 %): a freeze from an unaware thread took 46–57 ms (debug build) at the display's physical size; our window froze exact and was not offered; a window shielded by title before it showed stayed out of 10 freezes, then showed once unshielded.
- Task 1's shield probes now use the real shield: a shown window left the next grab after the call in 13–15 ms, no frame waited; the title walk adds about 7 ms to Task 1's 5–8 ms.
- No other app's window was shown during the runs, so the live freeze offered none; the offer rules are tested over scripted looks. G24's live run and the hands-on check (task 22) see a real one.
- Fix round 1:
  - A click-through window (layered and transparent) is never offered, by the scroll target's own rule, so a full-screen overlay cannot take every Window pick.
  - A freeze is refused (`Refused(E_NOTIMPL)`) when the thread cannot switch to physical pixels. DWM's frame bounds are never scaled, but the screens, the pointer and a plain window box are; at 125 % an unaware thread reads 1536×864.
  - A refused shield lets every window with that title back in, so none stays half shielded.

### Task 8b findings (2026-11-24)

- `WorkerRecorder` runs each recording and each copy in a `record` job of its own, on a stream, capped at `job_mb`. A lost job ends the run with one `Fault(Gone)` and deletes its video (edge 13); the next start begins a new job.
- Before any job starts, the host checks: the port's ranges, the seal, the unsaved part (`NoFolder` in a hidden run), and `min_free_mb`. A copy also checks that its source is a listed video of the unsaved part.
- Videos are named `<unix seconds>.mp4`, counted on past every held name and the last one given. A pasted copy keeps this name, as task 13 left it.
- Waits (`[snip]` settings since Task 21's ruling 1, read into `RecordWaits`):
  - a start waits `record_start_ms` for its first note;
  - a run must tell its time within `record_tick_ms`;
  - a switch waits `switch_ms` by the clock;
  - Stop and Bye wait `record_end_ms`;
  - a copy, which cannot be cancelled, waits `speed_ms` plus `speed_mb_ms` per MB of its source.
- The host deletes the video:
  - after a start's fault;
  - after a fault mid-run, `Full` included;
  - after a lost job;
  - after an end that follows Cancel;
  - after a cancelled run is dropped before it ended.

  A video kept by Stop, `max_min` or the floor stays once its `Done` was told, even if Cancel comes later. A Cancel before the run's caller heard `Done` wins: no `Done` is told, and the video goes.
- A lost job's loss is told only once its worker exited or its deadline passed, so its files are usually closed; the stream waits one `deadline_ms` at most in all. A delete the folder refuses is tried again once the job ended; one still refused is left for the sweep, with a warning.
- Cancel is posted and never waits for the job. `start`, `stop`, `mic` and `sound` may wait seconds, so the snip actor calls them on its job threads.
- The host never tells `RecordError::Memory`; a job past its cap ends as `Gone`, as the port doc now says.
- `WorkerFileAsk` shows one dialog at a time in the `pick` job. It grants the worker the foreground (`AllowSetForegroundWindow`) before each dialog, waits up to `dialog_min`, and checks each answer again with `PickAsk::check`.
  - `Refused` maps to `BadPath`, which asks the user to choose another place (`snip.note-place-refused`, Task 21's ruling 3). `Failed` maps to `Failed`, with its code logged.
  - The pick job's cap is `pick_mb`; the probe peaked at 12 MB under it.
- A Save answer writes the PNG or copies a video of the unsaved part with `std::fs`, and a failed write maps to `Missing`, `ReadOnly`, `Full` or `Io`. Each write goes to a new `<name>.<process id>-<n>.kxpart` beside the target, never over a part file already there (the next `n`, up to `PART_TRIES` tries), and is renamed over the target only when whole, so a failed write leaves the place as it was.
- `DiskSnips` is the Snips folder: plain PNG and MP4 files of `unsaved/` with their ages, deletes only there, and the drive's room through `WinDisk`. The app makes the folders at boot, unless the run is hidden (task 17).
- `WinMediaOpen` plays an MP4 that `playable` takes: directly in the Snips folder or `unsaved/`, a plain file and never a link. The running Explorer opens it; tests use a scripted shell and never play anything.
- `WinKit` reads the facts without loading a codec:
  - Media Foundation's DLLs, as listed in `record.toml`, present in System32;
  - the default microphone and speakers, from the system's device list;
  - the three microphone privacy switches named in `kit.toml`.
- Per-job limits live in one table, `JOBS_UI`: every job keeps every UI limit, and memory comes from each `Launch`. The rights tests now start all seven jobs, and `start_lifted` and the probes' lift constants are gone.
- Live (`KX_LIVE_SNIP=1`):
  - The host kept a 2,033 ms run of 2.1 MB, made a 2× copy, and a cancelled run left no file.
  - The pick probe passed under the pick job's launch (`Launch::pick`, now capped at `pick_mb`).
  - The FileAsk host itself was not run live: finding the worker's dialog needs its process id, which the host hides. The hands-on check (task 22) covers it.
